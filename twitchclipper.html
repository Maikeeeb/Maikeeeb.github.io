The starting point

Why build it?

Watching a long recording to find useful moments is slow. Chat timestamps provide a measurable signal that can narrow the search, while leaving the final editorial decision to a person.

My contribution

What I built.

I built a pipeline around chat parsing, time buckets, spike detection, candidate scoring, selection, and ffmpeg cutting and montage output. A FastAPI service, job queue, and worker expose the processing flow; an early .NET WPF frontend explores desktop access.

For a closer technical review

Decisions & tradeoffs.

Open each note for the implementation boundary, tradeoff, and next engineering question.

01Keep scoring independent of I/O

The chat-spike module computes buckets and threshold matches in memory. Typed models reject invalid timestamps and segment ranges before downstream processing. This makes selection logic testable without downloading a VOD or launching ffmpeg.

02Jobs make long work observable

The service queues work rather than rendering inside the request. In-memory and SQLite-backed storage serve different persistence needs. Process crashes, partial files, and retry safety become central when promoting a local tool into a service.

Scope & next steps

What this demonstrates.

Chat spikes can reflect spam, confusion, or delayed reactions. They are a ranking signal, not a promise of good highlights. Downloads, third-party changes, and desktop integration need separate operational validation.
